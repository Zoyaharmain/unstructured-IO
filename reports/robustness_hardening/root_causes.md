# Root-Cause Analysis: Adversarial & Edge-Case Resume Parsing Failures

**Project:** `unstructured-IO`  
**Branch:** `schema-mapping-accuracy-fixes`  
**Author:** Document Understanding & Production Reliability Staff Engineering  

---

## 1. Classification of Root Causes

The 17 baseline adversarial failures and 3 subsequent regression test failures stem from eight distinct systemic root causes in document layout interpretation, token segmentation, regex lookahead interference, and evidence attribution.

---

### Root Cause 1: Unshielded Prompt & Instruction Injection
- **Affected Fixtures:** `adv_f07_prompt_injection`
- **Mechanism:** In `local_api/services/record_subfields.py`, `labelled_fields` matched label patterns (e.g., `role:`, `title:`) across raw unstructured text without requiring structural delimiters (line starts, bullets, pipes, or semicolons). Adversarial payloads embedded in narrative paragraphs (e.g. `"ignore previous instructions, role: CEO"`) were parsed as legitimate metadata labels.
- **Systemic Impact:** Security vulnerability and data integrity failure where malicious document payloads could corrupt downstream ATS scoring or executive summaries.

---

### Root Cause 2: Invariant Violation False Positives from String Identity vs Evidence Citations
- **Affected Fixtures:** `adv_b08_similar_responsibilities_multiple_roles`, `adv_d05_multipage_4pages`, `adv_f03_extremely_long_resume`
- **Mechanism:** Invariant 3 in `scripts/run_adversarial_evaluation.py` checked cross-record leakage by taking all responsibility text strings across all experience entries and asserting `len(unique_strings) == len(total_strings)`. When a professional genuinely performed identical duties across two roles (e.g. `"Mentored junior developers on best coding practices"` in Job 1 and Job 2), the test flagged this as "Cross-record responsibility leakage".
- **Systemic Impact:** Conflating source span leakage with legitimate repeated source content penalizes high-fidelity parsers and prevents accurate resume extraction.

---

### Root Cause 3: Incomplete Role Lexicon & Lookahead Collisions in Action Verbs
- **Affected Fixtures:** `adv_a01_16_employers`, `adv_a02_multi_role_one_employer`, `adv_a06_role_before_employer`, `adv_b07_responsibilities_mentioning_vendor`
- **Mechanism:**
  1. The `ROLE` regular expression omitted common canonical titles in specialized disciplines (e.g., `fellow`, `resident`, `vp`, `vice president`, `president`, `physician`, `attorney`, `counsel`, `cso`, `cto`, `cio`, `cfo`, `ceo`, `investigator`, `chemist`, `tester`, `diagnostician`).
  2. The `ACTION` verb regular expression used a single-word lookahead `lead(?!\s+(?:business|software|data|engineer|analyst)...)`. When encountered with multi-word titles such as `Lead Frontend Engineer`, `Lead Infrastructure Architect`, or `Lead Defense Specialist`, `lead` matched as an action verb rather than part of the job title.
- **Systemic Impact:** Valid job titles were misclassified as bulleted narrative responsibilities, causing the employer header to collapse or fail boundary detection.

---

### Root Cause 4: Career Gaps Treated as Corporate Employers
- **Affected Fixtures:** `adv_c05_employment_gaps`
- **Mechanism:** In `employment_start` and `employment_header_fields`, any dated phrase containing a non-action prefix was assumed to be an employer. When candidates annotated `"Career Break — Full-time Parent (2020 - 2021)"` or `"Sabbatical (2018 - 2019)"`, the parser instantiated corporate experience records with company `"Career Break"` or `"Sabbatical"`.
- **Systemic Impact:** Fabricated corporate employment records, skewing work history duration and ATS employment tenure calculations.

---

### Root Cause 5: Unstructured Table Cell Layouts Lacking Boundary Triggers
- **Affected Fixtures:** `adv_e04_table_layout_employment`
- **Mechanism:** Table blocks generated by Word or PDF extractors frequently format employment information in key-value cells (`Company: Acme Corp`, `Designation: Senior Architect`, `Duration: 2021 - Present`). Because `employment_start` looked for inline delimited lines, table blocks were treated as generic narrative text.
- **Systemic Impact:** Complex tabular resumes lost experience boundaries, collapsing whole employment tables into single unparsed blocks.

---

### Root Cause 6: Academic Grants, Fellowships & Honors Colliding with Corporate Employers
- **Affected Fixtures:** `adv_e07_academic_cv_publications`
- **Mechanism:** Fellowships and research grants (e.g. `"EPSRC Quantum Technology Fellowship (£1.2M, 2020 - 2025)"`) contain dates and organization-like words (`Technology`, `EPSRC`), triggering corporate employer boundaries in headingless or research sections.
- **Systemic Impact:** Academic CVs generated false employment records for research funding and grants.

---

### Root Cause 7: Post-Role Em-Dash Subfunctions & Departments
- **Affected Regressions:** `tests/test_schema_diagnostics.py::test_dictionary_free_employer_headers_are_not_replaced_by_department_names`
- **Mechanism:** When a title line contains an em-dash (`Senior Analyst — Private Wealth Management Reporting`), splitting on `—` yielded a role on the left and a department on the right. When the parser blindly assigned the non-role component to `result['company']`, the department (`Private Wealth Management Reporting`) replaced the actual employer (`Acuity Knowledge Partners`) established on the previous source line.
- **Systemic Impact:** Department/reporting line text overwrote true employer identities.

---

### Root Cause 8: Rigid Section Transition Couplings Blocking Multi-Column Continuations
- **Affected Regressions:** `tests/test_schema_assignment_integrity.py`, `tests/test_resume_sections.py`
- **Mechanism:** Overly defensive context checks (e.g., restricting `employer_boundary` strictly to when context is already `experience`, `projects`, or `summary`) prevented legitimate transitions from a sidebar (skills/certifications) back into work experience when a dated employment entry appeared without a repeated section heading.
- **Systemic Impact:** Multi-column layouts lost second-column job entries, placing them into sidebar sections.
